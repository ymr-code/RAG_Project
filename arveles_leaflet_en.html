ARVELES® 25 mg Film-Coated Tablet — Package Leaflet

For oral use.

Composition

  • Active substance: 36.9 mg dexketoprofen trometamol, equivalent to 25 mg dexketoprofen.
  • Excipients: microcrystalline cellulose, corn starch, sodium starch glycolate, glycerol distearate, hypromellose, titanium dioxide, macrogol 6000, propylene glycol.

1. What ARVELES is and what it is used for

What ARVELES is

ARVELES is a painkiller belonging to the group of medicines called non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).

What ARVELES is used for

It is used for the treatment of the signs and symptoms of osteoarthritis (joint wear and degeneration), rheumatoid arthritis (inflammatory joint disease), and ankylosing spondylitis (a painful, progressive rheumatic disease mostly affecting the spinal joints, causing stiffness), as well as for acute gouty arthritis (painful joint inflammation caused by gout), acute musculoskeletal pain (e.g. lower back pain), postoperative pain (pain after surgery), and dysmenorrhea (painful menstrual periods).

Physical description and packaging

ARVELES 25 mg is a white, round, convex, scored film-coated tablet containing dexketoprofen (as trometamol), available in packs of 20 and 30 tablets. The tablets can be split into two equal halves.

2. What you need to know before you use ARVELES

Do NOT use ARVELES if:

  • You are allergic (hypersensitive) to dexketoprofen trometamol or any of the excipients of ARVELES.
  • You are allergic to acetylsalicylic acid or other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
  • You have asthma, or you have previously experienced an asthma attack, acute allergic rhinitis (short-term allergic inflammation inside the nose), nasal polyps (allergy-related masses inside the nose), urticaria (skin rash), swelling of the face and throat due to allergy (swelling of the face, eyes, lips, or tongue, or difficulty breathing), or wheezing after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs.
  • You have experienced photoallergic or phototoxic reactions (typical redness and spotting on skin exposed to sunlight) during use of ketoprofen (an NSAID) or fibrates (medicines used to lower blood fat levels).
  • You have a peptic ulcer (stomach or intestinal ulcer), stomach or intestinal bleeding, or ongoing digestive problems (e.g. indigestion, heartburn), or you have previously had stomach or intestinal bleeding, ulcer, or perforation.
  • You have ongoing digestive system problems (for example, indigestion, heartburn).
  • You have previously had stomach or intestinal bleeding or perforation related to the use of NSAID painkillers.
  • You have ongoing inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis).
  • You have severe heart failure, moderate or severe kidney problems, or severe liver problems.
  • You have a bleeding disorder or a blood clotting disorder.
  • You are severely dehydrated (major fluid loss from the body) due to vomiting, diarrhea, or insufficient fluid intake.
  • You are in the last three months of pregnancy, or you are breastfeeding.
  • You have undergone coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery — do not use for pain treatment before, during, or after the surgery.

Take special care with ARVELES if:

  • You have allergies or a history of allergic problems.
  • You have kidney, liver, or heart problems (high blood pressure and/or heart failure), including fluid retention, or you have had any of these problems in the past.
  • You are taking diuretic (water pill) medicines, or you have fluid deficiency and reduced blood volume due to excessive fluid loss (e.g. excessive urination, diarrhea, or vomiting).
  • You have heart-related problems, have previously had a stroke, or believe you may be at risk (e.g. if you have high blood pressure, diabetes, or high cholesterol, or if you smoke) — consult your doctor or pharmacist about your treatment. Medicines like ARVELES may be associated with a slight increase in the risk of heart attack ("myocardial infarction") or stroke. Any such risk is more likely with high doses and prolonged treatment. Do not exceed the recommended dose or duration of treatment.
  • You are elderly: you are more likely to experience side effects (see Section 4). If any of these occur, consult your doctor immediately.
  • You are a woman with fertility problems (ARVELES may adversely affect fertility, so do not use this medicine if you are planning to become pregnant or are undergoing fertility testing).
  • You have a disorder of blood or blood cell production.
  • You have systemic lupus erythematosus (a disease presenting with widespread skin scaling) or mixed connective tissue disease (immune system disorders that can affect connective tissue).
  • Very rarely, some fatal serious skin reactions have been reported, including exfoliative dermatitis (a skin condition with redness and peeling over large areas of the body), Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and toxic epidermal necrolysis (a skin condition with redness and peeling over large areas of the body). ARVELES must be discontinued at the first sign of skin rash, mucosal lesions, or any other sign of hypersensitivity.
  • You have previously had inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease).
  • You have, or have previously had, another stomach or intestinal condition.
  • You have an infection — please see the "Infections" section below.

Stomach and intestinal risks

NSAIDs can cause serious, potentially fatal gastrointestinal side effects such as bleeding, ulceration, or perforation of the stomach or intestine. These side effects can occur at any time, with or without warning signs. Elderly patients carry a higher risk of serious gastrointestinal side effects.

If you are taking medicines that increase the risk of peptic ulcer or bleeding — e.g. oral steroids, certain antidepressants (the SSRI group; Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors), or anticoagulant medicines such as aspirin or warfarin that prevent blood clotting — please consult your doctor before taking ARVELES. Your doctor may prescribe additional medicines to protect your stomach (e.g. misoprostol or acid-suppressing medicines).

You have a higher risk of allergy to acetylsalicylic acid and/or NSAIDs than the general population if you have asthma combined with chronic rhinitis, chronic sinusitis, and/or nasal polyps caused by the use of acetylsalicylic acid or other NSAIDs. Use of this medicine may cause asthma attacks or bronchospasm (narrowing of the airways), particularly in patients allergic to acetylsalicylic acid or NSAIDs.

Like other NSAIDs, ARVELES may mask the symptoms of infectious diseases. For this reason, blood counts may be performed to monitor for porphyria (a rare disease affecting the skin or nervous system that causes abdominal pain) and for signs of GI (digestive system) bleeding.

If any of the above warnings apply to you — even if only in the past — please consult your doctor before taking ARVELES.

Infections

ARVELES may mask signs of infection, such as fever and pain. It is therefore possible that ARVELES may delay appropriate treatment of an infection, which can increase the risk of complications. This has been observed in bacterial pneumonia and in bacterial skin infections related to chickenpox. If you take this medicine while you have an infection and your infection symptoms persist or worsen, consult a doctor without delay.

Kounis syndrome

Symptoms of an allergic reaction to this medicine — such as breathing problems, swelling of the face and neck area (angioedema), and chest pain — have been reported with dexketoprofen. If you notice any of these symptoms, stop taking Arveles immediately and seek urgent medical attention.

It is recommended to avoid using this medicine during chickenpox.

Children and adolescents

ARVELES has not been studied in children and adolescents. For this reason, since safety and efficacy have not been established, it must not be used in children and adolescents.

Using ARVELES with food and drink

Take the tablets with an adequate amount of water. Taking the tablets with food helps reduce the risk of side effects that may occur in the stomach or intestine. However, if you have acute pain, take the tablet on an empty stomach; taking the medicine at least 30 minutes before meals helps it act a little faster.

Pregnancy

If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, think you may be pregnant, or are planning to have a baby, ask your doctor or pharmacist for advice before taking this medicine.

Do not use ARVELES during the last three months of pregnancy. It may cause kidney and heart problems in your unborn baby. It may affect the bleeding tendency of both you and your baby, and may cause labor to start later than expected or to last longer.

Unless strictly necessary and advised by your doctor, you should not take ARVELES during the first six months of pregnancy. If treatment is needed during this period or while trying to conceive, the lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest possible time.

If ARVELES is taken for more than a few days from the 20th week of pregnancy onward, it may cause kidney problems in your unborn baby that can lead to low levels of the amniotic fluid surrounding the baby (oligohydramnios), or narrowing of a blood vessel in the baby's heart (ductus arteriosus). If you need treatment for longer than a few days, your doctor may recommend additional monitoring.

Use of ARVELES is not recommended while trying to conceive or during infertility treatment. Regarding potential effects on female fertility, see also Section 2.

If you become aware that you are pregnant during treatment, consult your doctor immediately.

Breastfeeding

Consult your doctor or pharmacist before taking this medicine. Do not use ARVELES if you are breastfeeding.

Driving and using machines

As a side effect of treatment, ARVELES may cause dizziness, visual disturbances, or drowsiness, which may impair your ability to react, to actively take part in road traffic, and to drive or operate machinery. If you notice these effects, do not drive or operate machinery until the symptoms have passed. Ask your doctor for advice.

Information about certain excipients in ARVELES

This medicinal product contains less than 1 mmol (23 mg) sodium per dose; that is to say, it is essentially "sodium-free".

Using other medicines

Be sure to inform your doctor, dentist, or pharmacist if you are using any of the following medicines alongside ARVELES. Some medicines should not be used together, or their doses may need to be adjusted if they are used together.

Combinations not recommended

  • Acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), corticosteroids (cortisone), or other anti-inflammatory drugs.
  • Warfarin, heparin, or other medicines used to prevent blood clotting.
  • Lithium, used to treat certain mood disorders.
  • Methotrexate, when used at doses higher than 15 mg per week for rheumatoid arthritis and cancer treatment.
  • Hydantoins and phenytoin, used for epilepsy.
  • Sulfamethoxazole, used for bacterial infections.

Combinations requiring precaution

  • ACE inhibitors and diuretics used for high blood pressure and heart problems.
  • Beta-blockers and angiotensin II antagonists.
  • Pentoxifylline and oxpentifylline, used to treat ongoing venous ulcers (leg vein sores).
  • Zidovudine, used for viral infection treatment.
  • Aminoglycoside antibiotics (gentamicin, amikacin, etc.), used for bacterial infections.
  • Sulfonylureas (e.g. chlorpropamide and glibenclamide), used for diabetes.
  • Methotrexate, when used at low doses (less than 15 mg per week).

Combinations to be taken into consideration

  • Quinolone antibiotics (e.g. ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin), used for bacterial infections.
  • Cyclosporine or tacrolimus, used in immune system disorders and organ transplantation.
  • Streptokinase and other thrombolytic or fibrinolytic medicines, such as those used to dissolve blood clots.
  • Probenecid, used in the treatment of gout.
  • Digoxin, used in the treatment of ongoing heart failure.
  • Mifepristone, used to terminate pregnancy.
  • Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) antidepressants.
  • Anti-platelet agents used to reduce platelet aggregation and blood clot formation.
  • Beta-blockers and diuretics (furosemide), used for high blood pressure and heart problems.
  • Tenofovir (used in antiviral treatment), deferasirox (used as an iron chelator), pemetrexed (used in cancer treatment).

Please tell your doctor or pharmacist if you are currently taking, or have recently taken, any other prescription or over-the-counter medicine.

3. How to use ARVELES

Instructions for proper use and dosage/frequency

Always take ARVELES exactly as your doctor has told you. Check with your doctor or pharmacist if you are not sure.

The lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest time necessary to relieve symptoms. If you have an infection and your symptoms (such as fever and pain) persist or worsen, consult a doctor without delay (see Section 2).

The dose of ARVELES you need depends on the type, severity, and duration of your pain. Your doctor will tell you how many tablets to take per day and for how long.

The recommended dose is generally 1 tablet (25 mg) every 8 hours, not exceeding 3 tablets (75 mg) per day.

Method of administration

Swallow the tablets with a sufficient amount of water.

If your pain is severe and you need faster relief, take the tablet on an empty stomach (at least 30 minutes before a meal), as it is absorbed more easily this way (see Section 2, "Using ARVELES with food and drink").

Different age groups

Use in children and adolescents: ARVELES must not be used in children and adolescents.

Use in the elderly: if you are elderly, you should start treatment at a dose not exceeding 2 tablets (50 mg) per day. If ARVELES is well tolerated, this starting dose may later be increased to the generally recommended dose (75 mg).

Special populations

Kidney/liver impairment: if you have mild kidney problems, or mild-to-moderate liver problems, you should start treatment at a dose not exceeding 2 tablets (50 mg) per day. ARVELES must not be used in patients with moderate or severe kidney dysfunction, or severe liver dysfunction.

If you feel that the effect of ARVELES is too strong or too weak, talk to your doctor or pharmacist.

If you use more ARVELES than you should

The symptoms that follow an overdose are not known. Similar medicinal products have caused gastrointestinal effects (vomiting, anorexia/loss of appetite, abdominal pain) and neurological effects (drowsiness, vertigo, disorientation/impaired sense of time and place, headache).

If you have used more ARVELES than you should, talk to a doctor or pharmacist, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Please remember to bring the medicine's packaging or this leaflet with you.

If you forget to use ARVELES

Take the next dose at its scheduled time. Do not take a double dose to make up for a missed dose.

Effects of stopping treatment with ARVELES

After ARVELES treatment is stopped, the condition generally returns to how it was before treatment.

Elderly patients are more likely to experience a decline in kidney function.

Like all NSAIDs, ARVELES may increase plasma urea nitrogen and creatinine levels (i.e. it may impair kidney function).

4. Possible side effects

Like all medicines, ARVELES can cause side effects, although not everybody gets them; side effects may also occur in people who are sensitive to the substances contained in ARVELES.

Frequency categories

Possible side effects are listed below according to how often they occur. This scale shows how many patients may experience each side effect:

  • Very common: may occur in at least 1 in 10 patients (frequency ≥ 1/10).
  • Common: may occur in fewer than 1 in 10 patients, but more than 1 in 100 patients (frequency ≥ 1/100 and < 1/10).
  • Uncommon: may occur in fewer than 1 in 100 patients, but more than 1 in 1,000 patients (frequency ≥ 1/1,000 and < 1/100).
  • Rare: may occur in fewer than 1 in 1,000 patients, but more than 1 in 10,000 patients (frequency ≥ 1/10,000 and < 1/1,000).
  • Very rare: may occur in fewer than 1 in 10,000 patients (frequency < 1/10,000).
  • Not known: frequency cannot be estimated from the available data.

Stop taking ARVELES and seek IMMEDIATE medical attention if you notice any of the following

If you notice any of the following, stop taking ARVELES and inform your doctor immediately, or go to the emergency department of the nearest hospital:

  • Skin rash, itching, bruising, painful red areas, skin peeling, mucosal lesions (tissue damage), or any other sign of hypersensitivity.
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding or ulceration.
  • Liver function test abnormalities that persist or worsen, clinical signs and symptoms consistent with liver disease, or other findings (e.g. eosinophilia, skin rash, etc.).
  • Serious acute hypersensitivity reactions (e.g. anaphylactic reactions), such as difficulty breathing with or without swelling of the face, lips, tongue, and/or throat.

All of these are very serious side effects. If you experience any of them, you may be having a serious allergic reaction to ARVELES. You may need urgent medical attention or hospitalization.

Side effects by frequency

Common side effects

  • Nausea and/or vomiting
  • Abdominal pain (usually in the upper abdomen)
  • Diarrhea
  • Digestive problems (indigestion)

Uncommon side effects

  • Dizziness (vertigo)
  • Light-headedness
  • Drowsiness
  • Sleep disturbances
  • Nervousness
  • Headache
  • Palpitations
  • Flushing
  • Inflammation of the stomach lining (gastritis)
  • Constipation
  • Dry mouth
  • Bloating
  • Skin rash
  • Fatigue
  • Pain
  • Fever and a feeling of chills
  • General weakness and shivering, usually a feeling of being generally unwell (malaise)

Rare side effects

  • Peptic ulcer (an ulcer in the stomach or duodenum)
  • Peptic ulcer perforation or bleeding, shown by vomiting blood or having black stools
  • Fainting
  • High blood pressure
  • Very slow breathing
  • Fluid retention and swelling in parts of the body away from the trunk (e.g. swollen ankles)
  • Loss of appetite (anorexia)
  • Abnormal sensations (e.g. tingling, numbness)
  • Swelling in the throat area (laryngeal oedema)
  • Sudden-onset kidney impairment (acute kidney failure)
  • Liver cell damage (hepatitis)
  • Itchy rash (urticaria)
  • Acne
  • Increased sweating
  • Back pain
  • Frequent urination
  • Menstrual irregularities
  • Prostate problems
  • Abnormal liver function test results (blood tests)

Very rare side effects

  • Anaphylactic reaction (a very severe, sudden allergic response of the body)
  • Open sores on the skin, mouth, eyes, and genital areas (Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis/Lyell's syndrome)
  • Swelling of the face, or swelling of the lips and throat (angioneurotic oedema)
  • Breathlessness due to narrowing of the airways (bronchospasm)
  • Shortness of breath
  • Rapid heartbeat
  • Low blood pressure
  • Inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis)
  • Blurred vision
  • Ringing in the ears (tinnitus)
  • Skin tenderness
  • Sensitivity to light (photosensitivity)
  • Itching
  • Kidney problems
  • Low white blood cell count (neutropenia), low platelet count (thrombocytopenia)

Side effects with unknown frequency

  • Chest pain, which may be a sign of a potentially serious allergic reaction called Kounis syndrome.
  • Fixed drug eruption: an allergic skin reaction that can present with round or oval red patches, skin swelling, blistering, and itching. Darkening of the affected skin may also occur and can persist after healing. Fixed drug eruption typically reappears in the same location(s) if the medicine is taken again.

Additional notes on side effects

If you have previously experienced a side effect from long-term use of anti-inflammatory drugs — and especially if you are elderly — inform your doctor if you notice any gastrointestinal side effect at the start of treatment (e.g. abdominal pain, heartburn, bleeding).

Stop using ARVELES as soon as you notice a skin rash, a lesion inside the mouth or genital area, or any sign of allergy.

Fluid retention and swelling (particularly in the ankles and legs), raised blood pressure, and heart failure have been reported during treatment with NSAIDs. Medicines like ARVELES may be associated with a slight increase in the risk of heart attack ("myocardial infarction") or stroke.

In patients with immune system disorders affecting connective tissue (systemic lupus erythematosus or mixed connective tissue disease), anti-inflammatory drugs may rarely cause fever, headache, and neck stiffness.

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal. Peptic ulcers, perforation, or bleeding in the stomach or intestine — sometimes fatal, particularly in the elderly — may occur. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, bloating, constipation, indigestion, abdominal pain, melena (black stools), hematemesis (vomiting blood), ulcerative stomatitis (mouth sores), colitis, and worsening of Crohn's disease have been reported after use. Gastritis (inflammation of the stomach lining) has been observed less frequently.

As with other NSAIDs, hematological reactions (purpura, aplastic and hemolytic anemia, and rarely agranulocytosis and medullary hypoplasia) may occur.

If signs of infection appear or worsen while using ARVELES, inform your doctor immediately.

If any side effect becomes serious, or if you notice any side effect not listed in this leaflet, please inform your doctor or pharmacist.

Reporting side effects

If you experience any side effect, whether or not it is listed in this leaflet, talk to your doctor, pharmacist, or nurse. You can also report side effects directly to the Turkish Pharmacovigilance Center (TÜFAM) by using the "Medicine Side Effect Reporting" icon at www.titck.gov.tr, or by calling the side effect reporting line at 0 800 314 00 08. By reporting side effects, you help provide more information about the safety of this medicine.

5. How to store ARVELES

  • Keep ARVELES out of the sight and reach of children, in its original packaging.
  • Store below 25°C, protected from light.
  • Use in accordance with the expiry date.
  • Do not use ARVELES after the expiry date stated on the packaging.
  • Do not use ARVELES if you notice any defects in the product and/or its packaging.
  • Do not dispose of expired or unused medicines via wastewater or household waste. Hand them over to the collection system specified by the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change.
